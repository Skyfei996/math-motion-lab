# Moving Pens: A Multiples Problem — Teacher Script

- Grade: 4
- Lesson path: `lessons/钢笔搬家倍数问题/index.html`
- Suggested duration: 8–10 minutes
- Default values: Ning 30 pens, Qing 15 pens, target 8 times
- Teacher cue: Text under **Action** is for the teacher and should not be read aloud. Ask first, pause for student thinking, and then use **Next** to reveal the next visual and calculation.

## Stop 1: Read

**Action**

Open the lesson on page 1, “Read.” Point to the left-facing arrow between Qing and Ning.

**Ask**

Whose pencil case do the pens leave, and whose pencil case do they enter?

**Pause**

Wait for students to say, “Qing gives pens to Ning.” Then ask how each child's count will change.

**Say**

Qing gives some pens to Ning. Ning's number will go up, and Qing's number will go down. We are not guessing the amount yet. First, make sure the direction is clear.

**Screen check**

Ning has 30 pens on the left, Qing has 15 on the right, and the arrow points from Qing to Ning. No final answer is visible.

## Stop 2: Same total

**Action**

Select **Next** and point to the combined total bar.

**Ask**

How many pens do they have altogether? Does changing owners change the total?

**Pause**

Wait for students to add 30 and 15 and explain that no pens were added or lost.

**Say**

Qing is only handing pens to Ning. No pen leaves the pair, and no new pen appears. Their combined total stays the same. Thirty plus fifteen is forty-five, so they have 45 pens altogether.

**Screen check**

The stage joins 30 and 15 into a bar of 45 pens. The blackboard shows “30 + 15 = 45” and “Total = 45 pens.”

## Stop 3: See the multiple

**Action**

Select **Next**. Point across the eight teal parts and then the one gold part before drawing attention to the blackboard.

**Ask**

Ning has 8 parts and Qing has 1 part. How many equal parts are there altogether?

**Pause**

Wait for “9 parts.” If a student says 8, point again to the one part that Qing keeps.

**Say**

“Ning has eight times as many as Qing” means Qing's final amount is one part and Ning has eight parts of the same size. Together, they have eight parts plus one more part, which makes nine parts.

**Screen check**

The stage shows 8 teal parts for Ning and 1 gold part for Qing. The blackboard shows “8 parts + 1 part = 9 parts.” The transfer amount is still hidden.

## Stop 4: Find one part

**Action**

Select **Next** and let students inspect the 5 pens shown in every equal part.

**Ask**

If 45 pens are shared equally among 9 parts, how many pens are in each part? Whose final amount is one part?

**Pause**

Wait for students to calculate 45 divided by 9 and identify Qing's final one part.

**Say**

Forty-five pens shared equally among nine parts gives five pens in each part. Qing has one part at the end, so Qing keeps 5 pens. Ning has eight of those equal parts.

**Screen check**

All nine parts show 5 pens. The blackboard shows “45 ÷ 9 = 5” and “Qing ends with 5 pens.”

## Stop 5: Find the moved part

**Action**

Select **Next**. Point to Qing's starting 15 pens, the retained 5 pens, and the transfer packet marked with a question mark.

**Ask**

Qing starts with 15 pens and keeps 5. How many pens are given away?

**Pause**

Give students time to subtract. Listen to the answer before revealing it on the next page.

**Say**

To find the part given away, subtract the amount Qing keeps from the amount Qing started with. The blackboard still shows a question mark, so finish “fifteen minus five” yourselves.

**Screen check**

The transfer packet shows “? pens.” The blackboard shows “15 − 5 = ?” and “Pens given to Ning = ?.” The answer has not been formally revealed.

## Stop 6: Check

**Action**

Select **Next**. Point in order to the subtraction, both final counts, the multiple check, and the green answer strip.

**Ask**

After the move, does Ning really have 8 times as many pens as Qing? Is their total still 45?

**Pause**

Wait for students to check both 40 divided by 5 and 40 plus 5.

**Say**

Fifteen minus five is ten, so Qing gives away 10 pens. Ning has thirty plus ten, which is 40. Qing has fifteen minus ten, which is 5. Forty divided by five is eight, and forty plus five is still forty-five. Both checks work, so Qing gives Ning 10 pens.

**Screen check**

The stage shows Ning with 40 pens and Qing with 5. The blackboard shows “15 − 5 = 10,” “30 + 10 = 40,” “15 − 10 = 5,” and “40 ÷ 5 = 8.” The green strip states that Qing gives Ning 10 pens.

## Notes for changed values

- Use **Edit problem** to change Ning's starting count, Qing's starting count, or the target multiple.
- After selecting **Apply and restart**, the lesson returns to page 1 and recalculates all six stops.
- If the total cannot split evenly into “target multiple + 1” parts, or if the values require Ning to give pens to Qing, the dialog explains the mismatch. Do not continue with the six-stop script.
- For any other valid set of values, read the current numbers shown on the page while keeping the same reasoning sequence.
