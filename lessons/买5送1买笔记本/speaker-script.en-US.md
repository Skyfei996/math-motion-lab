# “Buy 5, Get 1 Notebook Deal” Teacher Script

- Grade: Grade 3
- Lesson path: `lessons/买5送1买笔记本/index.html`
- Suggested duration: 8–10 minutes
- Default values: 34 students, 6 yuan per notebook, buy 5 and get 1 free
- Teacher cues: “Action” names the exact page operation. Pause for student thinking before moving on. Editing the values restarts the lesson at page 1. When a conditional branch appears, read only the branch that matches the screen.

## Stop 1: Read

**Action**

Open “Buy 5, Get 1 Notebook Deal” from the catalog. Point to the class order and the store-deal sign on the stage.

**Ask**

What information is given, and what are we finding?

**Pause**

Wait for students to identify 34 students, one notebook each, 6 yuan per notebook, and the buy-5-get-1 deal. They should also name both questions: the least paid count and the total cost.

**Say**

The class monitor needs one notebook for each of 34 students, so the class needs 34 notebooks altogether. Each notebook costs 6 yuan, and the store offers one free notebook for every five paid notebooks. We are not finding how many notebooks leave the store—we already know the class needs 34. We are finding how many notebooks must be paid for, then the cost of those paid notebooks.

**Screen check**

The stage shows the full problem, including 34 students, one notebook each, 6 yuan each, the buy-5-get-1 deal, and both questions. No method or answer is shown.

## Stop 2: Deal

**Action**

Click “Next.” Point to the five orange paid notebooks and one green free notebook in the tray.

**Ask**

If we pay for 5 notebooks, how many notebooks do we receive?

**Pause**

Wait while students count the five paid notebooks and the one free notebook.

**Say**

“Buy 5, get 1” means we pay for five notebooks and the store adds one free notebook. Put them in one deal group. The group contains six notebooks, but only five are paid for.

**Screen check**

The tray shows five orange paid notebooks and one green free notebook. The board shows “5 + 1 = 6 notebooks.”

## Stop 3: Group

**Action**

Click “Next.” Point to the five complete trays and the four notebooks in the remainder area.

**Ask**

How many complete groups of six fit into 34, and how many notebooks remain?

**Pause**

Wait for students to name six as the size of one complete group before writing the division.

**Say**

Each complete deal group gives us six notebooks, so divide 34 by 6. Five groups cover 30 notebooks, with four notebooks left. We do not divide by 5 here: five is the paid part, while six is the number the class receives from one complete deal group.

**Screen check**

The stage shows five complete groups and four remaining notebooks. The board shows “34 ÷ 6 = 5 remainder 4.”

## Stop 4: Full groups

**Action**

Click “Next.” Point out the orange and green notebooks inside each tray.

**Ask**

How many notebooks are paid for in five complete deal groups?

**Pause**

Wait for students to explain that only five notebooks in each group are paid for.

**Say**

Each group gives us six notebooks, but the green notebook is free. We pay for five notebooks in each of five groups. Five times five is 25, so the complete groups account for 25 paid notebooks.

**Screen check**

All five trays keep the same five-paid-plus-one-free structure. The board shows “5 × 5 = 25 paid notebooks.”

## Stop 5: Remainder

**Action**

Click “Next.” Trace the scene from the paid notebooks in the complete groups, through the remaining notebooks, to the least paid count.

**Ask**

How many of the last four notebooks must be paid for?

**Pause**

Wait for students to decide that four does not trigger another free notebook.

**Say**

The complete groups account for 25 paid notebooks. The last four do not reach another group of five, so all four must be paid for. Twenty-five plus four is 29. The least paid count is 29 notebooks.

**Screen check**

The stage shows the quantity path “25 + 4 = 29.” The board shows “25 + 4 = 29 paid notebooks.”

**Conditional branch: no remainder**

If the screen shows a remainder of zero, the complete deal groups already meet the full need. No extra paid notebooks are added; read the displayed “complete-group paid count + 0” equation.

**Conditional branch: remainder equals the paid group size**

If the remainder equals the buy threshold, all of those notebooks are paid for and they trigger one more free notebook. The store may hand over one extra notebook, but the paid count is still “complete-group paid count + remainder.”

## Stop 6: Cost

**Action**

Click “Next.” Point to “Paid notebooks,” “Price each,” and “Total due” on the receipt.

**Ask**

If we pay for 29 notebooks at 6 yuan each, what is the total cost?

**Pause**

Wait for students to write the multiplication and explain why 34 is not multiplied by 6.

**Say**

The store charges for the 29 paid notebooks. The five free notebooks cost nothing. Twenty-nine times six is 174 yuan. We do not multiply 34 by 6 because 34 is the total number needed, not the number being charged.

**Screen check**

The receipt shows 29 paid notebooks, 6 yuan each, and 174 yuan total. The board shows “29 × 6 = 174 yuan.”

## Stop 7: Verify

**Action**

Click “Next.” Point first to the green “Enough” row, then to the red “One fewer is not enough” row.

**Ask**

Why is paying for 29 enough, and why is 29 the least possible paid count?

**Pause**

Wait for students to check both paying for 29 and paying for only 28.

**Say**

Twenty-nine contains five complete paid groups of five, so the store gives five free notebooks. Twenty-nine plus five is 34, exactly enough for the class. If we pay for only 28, the store still gives five free notebooks, but 28 plus 5 is only 33. That is one short. So 29 is enough and one fewer is not. Answer: pay for at least 29 notebooks. The total cost is 174 yuan.

**Screen check**

The green row shows “29 notebooks + 5 free = 34.” The red row shows “28 notebooks + 5 free = 33 < 34.” The complete answer appears only on this page.

**Conditional branch: one extra notebook**

If the current values trigger another free notebook at the final paid count, the green row shows one more notebook than the class needs. The red row must still show that paying for one fewer is not enough, proving that the current paid count is the least.

## Notes for changed values

- Click “Edit problem” to change the student count, price per notebook, and the number that must be bought to receive one free notebook. Click “Apply and restart” to return to page 1.
- A complete group is always “paid group size + 1.” The page recalculates the full groups, remainder, paid count, free count, total cost, and one-fewer check.
- Test 30 students, 6 yuan each, buy 5 get 1: there is no remainder, 25 notebooks are paid for, and the cost is 150 yuan.
- Test 35 students, 6 yuan each, buy 5 get 1: paying for 30 earns 6 free notebooks, so the store hands over 36. Paying for 29 gives only 34, so 30 is still the least paid count.
