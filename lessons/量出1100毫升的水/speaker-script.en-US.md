# Measure 1100 mL of Water — Teacher Script

- Grade: 4
- Lesson path: `lessons/量出1100毫升的水/index.html`
- Suggested duration: 8–10 minutes
- Optional exploration: about 6–8 additional minutes
- Default values: Bottle A 500 mL, Bottle B 800 mL, target 1100 mL
- Teacher cue: “Action” describes what the teacher does on the page. “One operation” in exploration mode follows the counting rule stated in that section. At each “Pause,” wait for student thinking before continuing.

Parameter note: The page automatically calls the larger container the “large bottle” and the smaller one the “small bottle.” If Bottle A is larger, read the Bottle A branch shown on screen; if Bottle B is larger, read the Bottle B branch. Use only the capacities currently displayed. This lesson accepts targets that equal the large bottle plus the difference between the two bottles.

## Stop 1: Read the problem

**Action**

Open “Measure 1100 mL of Water” from the catalog and stay on the “Read” page.

**Ask**

What do we know, and how much water must we measure?

**Pause**

Wait for students to identify a basin of water, an empty basin, a 500 mL bottle, an 800 mL bottle, and the 1100 mL target.

**Say**

We have one basin of water and one empty basin. The bottles hold 500 milliliters and 800 milliliters. They have no extra markings. Our job is to use only these two bottles to put exactly 1100 milliliters into the empty basin. We are reading the problem first, not pouring yet.

**Screen check**

The stage shows the complete problem, both basins, both bottles, and the 1100 mL target. It does not show 300 mL or the final method.

## Stop 2: Fill the large bottle

**Action**

Click “Next” to open “Fill the large bottle,” then let the water-level animation finish.

**Ask**

Which bottle should we fill first?

**Pause**

Let students identify the 800 mL bottle and watch its water level rise from empty to full.

**Say**

First, take water from the source basin and fill the 800 mL bottle. The large bottle now holds exactly 800 mL. We have not poured into the small bottle or the measuring basin yet.

**Screen check**

The animation plays once. When it ends, the 800 mL bottle is full and the blackboard says “Large bottle full: 800 mL.”

## Stop 3: Fill the small bottle

**Action**

Click “Next” to open “Fill the small bottle,” then let the large-to-small pouring animation finish.

**Ask**

The large bottle starts with 800 mL. How much remains after the 500 mL bottle is full?

**Pause**

Let students watch the small bottle fill and the large bottle’s water level fall. Then invite a mental subtraction.

**Say**

Use the large bottle to fill the 500 mL bottle and stop when the small bottle is full. Eight hundred minus five hundred is three hundred, so 300 mL remains in the large bottle.

**Screen check**

When the animation ends, the small bottle is full with 500 mL and the large bottle holds 300 mL. The blackboard reads `800 − 500 = 300 mL`.

## Stop 4: Pour into the measuring basin

**Action**

Click “Next” to open “Pour into the basin,” then let the large bottle pour its remaining 300 mL into the basin.

**Ask**

Once the basin has 300 mL, how much more is needed to reach 1100 mL?

**Pause**

When the animation ends, wait for students to calculate `1100 − 300` and state the result.

**Say**

Pour the 300 mL remaining in the large bottle into the measuring basin. We still need `1100 − 300 = 800` mL. That is exactly one full large bottle.

**Screen check**

When the animation ends, the large bottle is empty and the basin holds 300 mL. The stage highlights “800 mL still needed—exactly one full large bottle.” The blackboard shows `800 − 500 = 300` and `1100 − 300 = 800 mL`.

## Stop 5: Fill the large bottle again

**Action**

Click “Next” to open “Refill the large bottle,” then let its water level rise to 800 mL.

**Ask**

We still need 800 mL. Which bottle should we fill?

**Pause**

Wait for students to identify the 800 mL bottle, then let the filling animation finish.

**Say**

Fill the 800 mL bottle again. Leave the 300 mL already in the basin where it is. This full large bottle is exactly the 800 mL still needed.

**Screen check**

When the animation ends, the basin still holds 300 mL and the large bottle is full with 800 mL. The blackboard shows “Still needed: 800 mL” and “Large bottle full: 800 mL.”

## Stop 6: Pour the large bottle into the basin

**Action**

Click “Next” to open “Pour the large bottle,” then let the bottle-emptying and basin-filling animations finish.

**Ask**

The basin already has 300 mL. What is the total after adding 800 mL?

**Pause**

Wait for students to state `300 + 800 = 1100`, then summarize the complete method.

**Say**

Pour the full 800 mL bottle into the basin. When the bottle is empty, the basin holds `300 + 800 = 1100` mL. Answer: Put 300 mL into the empty basin first, then add one full 800 mL bottle to measure exactly 1100 mL.

**Screen check**

When the animation ends, the large bottle is empty and the water settles at about 61.8% of the basin’s usable height. This level represents 1100 mL; it does not imply that the basin’s capacity is 1100 mL. The page shows `800 − 500 = 300`, `300 + 800 = 1100 mL`, and the complete answer.

## Exploration: Why 100 mL is possible

**Action**

On the final page, click “Explore more pours.” Stay on the ready state before clicking “Next operation.”

**Ask**

If we keep comparing the differences made by 500 mL and 800 mL, what is the smallest amount we reach?

**Pause**

Guide students through `800 − 500 = 300`, `500 − 300 = 200`, and `300 − 200 = 100`.

**Say**

Both bottle capacities are multiples of 100 mL. Filling, emptying, and pouring between the bottles can only change the amounts in 100 mL steps. Therefore, 100 mL is their smallest common water unit. We can measure 100, 200, 300, and every other multiple of 100 mL. A target that is not a multiple of 100, such as 150 mL, cannot be measured exactly with these unmarked bottles.

**Screen check**

The page shows the three-part difference chain and “Smallest common water unit: 100 mL.” The default target is 100 mL. The board says “Minimum: 9 operations,” while all four containers remain in their initial state.

## Exploration: Measure 100 mL in nine minimum operations

**Action**

Click “Next operation” once after explaining each line, and let the water-level, bottle-tilt, and flow animations finish. On this page, one operation means filling one bottle, emptying one bottle back into the source, pouring between bottles, or pouring one bottle into the target basin.

**Route to narrate**

1. Fill the 800 mL bottle: small 0, large 800, basin 0.
2. Use it to fill the 500 mL bottle: small 500, large 300, basin 0.
3. Empty the small bottle back into the source: small 0, large 300, basin 0.
4. Pour the large bottle’s 300 mL into the small bottle: small 300, large 0, basin 0.
5. Fill the large bottle again: small 300, large 800, basin 0.
6. Use the large bottle to top up the small bottle: small 500, large 600, basin 0.
7. Empty the small bottle back into the source: small 0, large 600, basin 0.
8. Use the large bottle to fill the small bottle again: small 500, large 100, basin 0.
9. Pour the large bottle’s remaining 100 mL into the target basin: small 500, large 0, basin 100.

**Ask**

Why can the page call nine operations the minimum, rather than merely one method that happens to use nine operations?

**Pause**

Invite a prediction, then point to the “first arrival” explanation on the board.

**Say**

The page checks every state reachable in one operation, then every state reachable in two, then three, layer by layer. No route from zero through eight operations puts 100 mL into the target basin. The first arrival occurs at operation nine, so nine is the minimum.

**Screen check**

Each animation plays once, and the ending amounts match the route above. After operation nine, the target basin reads 100 mL and the board explains that every route through eight operations failed before the first arrival at nine.

## Exploration: Challenge another target

**Action**

Enter a target such as 200, 300, 1100, or 1400 in the “Measure” field. Click “Find the minimum,” then use “Next operation” to play the newly calculated shortest route.

**Ask**

Which targets can be measured exactly? Does a larger target always require more operations?

**Say**

An exact target must be a multiple of 100 mL. Once 100 mL can be measured, repeated measuring and saving in the target basin can build any multiple of 100. For a particular target, however, there may be a shorter route than repeating the 100 mL routine. For example, 1100 mL needs only the five operations from the main lesson. The minimum depends on useful full bottles and remainders, not only on the target’s size.

Entering 150 mL produces a message that the target is not a multiple of 100 and cannot be measured exactly. For every new valid target, the page recalculates the minimum count and every container state; these are not fixed answers.
