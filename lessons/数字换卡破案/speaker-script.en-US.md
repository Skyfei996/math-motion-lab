# Teacher Script: The Number-Card Mystery

- Grade: 4
- Lesson path: `lessons/数字换卡破案/index.html`
- Suggested duration: 12–15 minutes
- Default values: quotient 17, remainder 6, sum of dividend, divisor, quotient, and remainder 479
- Teacher note: Text under **Action** is a control cue and is not read aloud. Ask and pause before selecting “Next.” The card swap is a visual substitute for letter-based substitution.

## Stop 1: Read the mystery

**Action**

Open the lesson on page 1, “Read.” Point to the complete problem, then to each of the four number cards.

**Ask**

Which two numbers are known, and which two must we find?

**Pause**

Wait for students to identify quotient 17 and remainder 6, then state that the dividend and divisor are missing. Ask them to repeat that all four numbers add to 479.

**Say**

Four number cards are being added: the dividend, divisor, quotient, and remainder. The quotient and remainder cards are face up, but the dividend and divisor are still hidden. Together the four cards make 479. We will identify each card before doing any calculation.

**Screen check**

The stage shows every condition and the exact question. Both unknown cards show question marks, and no method or answer is revealed.

## Stop 2: Unpack the dividend

**Action**

Select “Next.” Point to the 17 matching divisor cards and the gold remainder card coming out of the “Division unpacker.”

**Ask**

If the quotient is 17 and the remainder is 6, what is packed inside the dividend?

**Pause**

Wait for “17 divisor groups and 6 more.” If students omit the leftover, point to the gold card.

**Say**

A quotient of 17 means that 17 complete divisor groups fit inside the dividend. After those groups are removed, 6 remains. So the dividend is not just 17 divisor groups. It is 17 divisor groups plus 6.

**Screen check**

The hidden dividend enters the unpacker. The output shows 17 equal divisor cards plus 6. The blackboard reads “Dividend = 17 × divisor + 6.”

## Stop 3: Swap the dividend card

**Action**

Select “Next.” Trace the original four-card sum, then follow the down arrow to the swapped version.

**Ask**

After replacing the dividend with its contents, what is now inside the four-number sum?

**Pause**

Have students read the new row from left to right: 17 divisor groups, 6, one divisor, quotient 17, and remainder 6.

**Say**

We still do not know the number printed on the dividend card, but we know exactly what it contains. We can replace the single card with those contents without changing the total. The total 479 now contains 17 divisor groups, the 6 inside the dividend, the original divisor card, the quotient 17, and the listed remainder 6.

**Screen check**

The swapped row reads “17 × divisor + 6 + divisor + 17 + 6 = 479.” The dividend question-mark card is gone, while the divisor remains unknown.

## Stop 4: Explain the two sixes

**Action**

Select “Next.” Point to gold cards ① and ② and read their source labels.

**Ask**

Where does each 6 come from? Did the division make two leftover piles?

**Pause**

Wait for students to distinguish the 6 inside the dividend from the remainder that is separately included in the four-number sum.

**Say**

The division did not create two leftover piles. The first 6 is part of the dividend because the dividend contains 17 divisor groups and 6 more. The second 6 is the remainder card itself, and the problem tells us to add that card as one of the four numbers. The same remainder clue affects the total in two places, which is why 6 appears twice after the swap.

**Screen check**

One card is labelled “Inside the dividend,” and the other is labelled “Remainder in the four-number sum.”

## Stop 5: Group matching cards

**Action**

Select “Next.” Count the teal divisor cards, ending on the blue-outlined original divisor card. Then point to the known-number basket.

**Ask**

Why are there 18 equal divisor cards now?

**Pause**

Wait for “17 came from the dividend, and one was already in the original sum.”

**Say**

The unpacked dividend gives us 17 divisor cards. The original four-number sum already contains one more divisor card. Because they are the same unknown number, they can stand together: 17 plus 1 makes 18 divisor cards. The remaining known numbers also stand together: 6 plus 17 plus 6 makes 29.

**Screen check**

The stage shows 18 equal divisor cards and identifies the original card with a blue outline. The known-number basket reads “6 + 17 + 6 = 29.”

## Stop 6: Remove the known numbers

**Action**

Select “Next.” Point to “18 equal divisor cards + 29 = 479,” then follow the arrow to 450.

**Ask**

After removing the known 29 from 479, what does the remaining 450 represent?

**Pause**

Wait for the complete statement: “450 is the value of 18 divisor cards together.”

**Say**

The total is now split into 18 equal divisor cards and the known number 29. Subtracting gives 479 minus 29, or 450. With every known number removed, the remaining 450 is made entirely of the 18 equal divisor cards.

**Screen check**

The blackboard reads “479 − 29 = 450” and “18 divisor cards together = 450.”

## Stop 7: Find the divisor

**Action**

Select “Next.” Watch all 18 cards reveal 25, then point to the remainder-rule check.

**Ask**

If 18 equal cards total 450, what is each card worth? Is remainder 6 valid?

**Pause**

Wait for students to calculate 450 divided by 18 and compare 6 with 25.

**Say**

All 18 divisor cards have the same value, so 450 divided by 18 is 25. The divisor is 25. In division with a remainder, the remainder must be smaller than the divisor. Six is less than 25, so the remainder rule is satisfied.

**Screen check**

Every divisor card shows 25. The blackboard reads “450 ÷ 18 = 25” and “6 < 25.”

## Stop 8: Rebuild the dividend

**Action**

Select “Next.” Point to the 17 cards worth 25, the remainder 6, and the rebuilt dividend card.

**Ask**

What is the dividend when we combine 17 groups of 25 and 6 more?

**Pause**

Wait for students to multiply 25 by 17, then add 6.

**Say**

The dividend contains 17 divisor groups. Each divisor is 25, so those groups total 425. Put the remaining 6 back in: 425 plus 6 is 431. The dividend is 431. We have found both hidden cards, but we will not give the complete answer until both checks pass.

**Screen check**

The stage shows “25 × 17 + 6 = 431” and reveals the dividend as 431. The full answer sentence is still hidden.

## Stop 9: Double-check

**Action**

Select “Next.” Point to each green check card, then read the answer banner.

**Ask**

Do 431 and 25 satisfy both the division clue and the four-number sum?

**Pause**

Have students check the division first and the sum second.

**Say**

The first lock checks the division: 431 divided by 25 gives quotient 17 and remainder 6. The second lock checks the total: 431 plus 25 plus 17 plus 6 equals 479. Both locks open, so the answer is confirmed: the dividend is 431, and the divisor is 25.

**Screen check**

The page shows “431 ÷ 25 = 17 remainder 6,” “431 + 25 + 17 + 6 = 479,” and the full answer for the first time.

## Parameter branches

- Select “Edit problem” to change the quotient, remainder, or four-number sum. “Apply and restart” returns the lesson to Stop 1 and recalculates all nine stops.
- When the remainder is zero, Stop 2 explains an exact division and Stop 4 distinguishes the sources of the two zero positions. The card-swap reasoning remains the same.
- If the swapped total cannot produce a whole-number divisor, or if the remainder is not smaller than the divisor, the settings dialog explains the conflict and does not create a false lesson state.
- For a large quotient, repeated cards are visually compressed with the exact total clearly labelled; the mathematics is not shortened.
