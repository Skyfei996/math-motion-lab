# Teacher Script: How Many Detergent Bags Were in Each Box?

- Grade: 4
- Lesson path: `lessons/甲乙两箱原来各有多少袋洗衣粉/index.html`
- Suggested duration: 10–12 minutes
- Default values: 90 bags in all; move 4 bags from Box A to Box B; Box A still has 6 more bags
- Teacher cue: Do not read the **Action** notes aloud. Ask first, pause for students, and then select **Next** so the screen performs one mathematical action.

## Parameter branch

If you use **Edit problem**, read the values currently shown on screen instead of the default numbers below. Every valid set of values follows the same 13-stop route:

1. subtract the final difference from the total;
2. share the remainder equally between two boxes;
3. restore the extra part to Box A;
4. reverse the moved amount;
5. check both the total and the final difference.

If the dialog says the values do not produce positive whole-bag answers, leave it open and read the correction message. Do not start the lesson with invalid values.

## Stop 1: Read the whole problem

**Action**

Open the lesson and leave it at **1 / 13**. Do not select **Next** yet.

**Ask**

What facts do we know, and what must we find?

**Pause**

Wait for students to restate the total, the direction of the move, the final difference, and the question.

**Say**

Box A and Box B hold 90 bags of detergent in all. Four bags move from Box A to Box B. After the move, Box A still has 6 more bags than Box B. We need to find how many bags were originally in each box.

**Screen check**

The stage shows all four parts of the problem: 90 bags in all, 4 moving from A to B, A still 6 ahead, and the original counts to be found. No method or answer is visible.

## Stop 2: The total stays the same

**Action**

Ask, pause, and then select **Next**.

**Ask**

If 4 bags only move from one box to the other, does the total change?

**Pause**

Wait for “no,” then ask whether any bag entered or left the pair of boxes.

**Say**

No bag entered or left. The bags only changed places, so the total is still 90.

**Screen check**

The top brace says “90 bags in all,” and the board says “Before and after: 90 bags in all.”

## Stop 3: Move 4 bags from A to B

**Action**

Ask, pause, and then select **Next**.

**Ask**

Where do the 4 bags start, and where do they go?

**Pause**

Wait for students to point from Box A to Box B.

**Say**

Watch the 4 bags leave Box A and enter Box B. Box A loses 4, Box B gains 4, and the total remains 90.

**Screen check**

The gold 4-bag bundle travels from A to B. The board reads “Box A → Box B: 4 bags.”

## Stop 4: See the final difference

**Action**

Ask, pause, and then select **Next**.

**Ask**

After the move, how many extra bags does Box A have?

**Pause**

Ask students to point to the unmatched segment in Box A.

**Say**

The green lengths can be matched. The red segment at the end of Box A is the 6-bag excess.

**Screen check**

Box A shows a red “6 extra bags” segment. The exact box counts are still hidden.

## Stop 5: Set aside the 6 extra bags

**Action**

Ask, pause, and then select **Next**.

**Ask**

If we set aside Box A’s 6 extra bags, how will the boxes compare?

**Pause**

Wait for students to compare the two green quantity bands and say they are equal.

**Say**

The extra 6 bags move above Box A. The green amount left in A is now the same length as all of Box B.

**Screen check**

The red segment lifts out of Box A. The two green bands are exactly equal, and the board says “Without 6 bags, the boxes are equal.”

## Stop 6: Find the total of the equal parts

**Action**

Ask, pause, and then select **Next**.

**Ask**

How many bags remain after removing the 6-bag excess from 90?

**Pause**

Wait for students to calculate 90 minus 6.

**Say**

Ninety minus 6 is 84. This is not one box’s amount. It is the combined total of the two equal parts.

**Screen check**

The board reads “90 − 6 = 84 bags.” The red segment remains set aside.

## Stop 7: Share 84 into two equal parts

**Action**

Ask, pause, and then select **Next**.

**Ask**

If 84 bags are shared equally into two parts, how many are in each part?

**Pause**

Wait for 42 and ask what one part represents.

**Say**

Eighty-four divided by 2 is 42. Box B has 42 bags after the move, and Box A also has a 42-bag equal part.

**Screen check**

Both boxes say “Equal part: 42 bags.” The board reads “84 ÷ 2 = 42 bags.”

## Stop 8: Restore Box A’s 6 extra bags

**Action**

Ask, pause, and then select **Next**.

**Ask**

Box B has 42 bags after the move. What does Box A have after we restore its 6 extra bags?

**Pause**

Wait for students to form 42 plus 6.

**Say**

Forty-two plus 6 is 48. After the move, Box A has 48 bags and Box B has 42 bags.

**Screen check**

The red segment returns to Box A. The labels show “After: 48 bags” and “After: 42 bags.”

## Stop 9: Work back to Box A’s original count

**Action**

Ask, pause, and then select **Next**.

**Ask**

Box A has 48 bags after the move. What was its original count when the moved 4 bags are returned?

**Pause**

Wait for students to explain why working backward uses addition.

**Say**

The problem took 4 bags away from Box A. To work backward, return them: 48 plus 4 is 52. Box A originally had 52 bags.

**Screen check**

The gold bundle returns to Box A. Box A says “At first: 52 bags,” and the board reads “48 + 4 = 52 bags.”

## Stop 10: Work back to Box B’s original count

**Action**

Ask, pause, and then select **Next**.

**Ask**

Box B has 42 bags after the move. What was its original count before it received 4 bags?

**Pause**

Wait for students to calculate 42 minus 4.

**Say**

Four of Box B’s 42 bags arrived later. Forty-two minus 4 is 38, so Box B originally had 38 bags.

**Screen check**

The original labels show 52 bags for A and 38 bags for B. The board reads “42 − 4 = 38 bags.”

## Stop 11: Check the original total

**Action**

Ask, pause, and then select **Next**.

**Ask**

Do the original counts, 52 and 38, add to the given total?

**Pause**

Let students add the two counts independently.

**Say**

Fifty-two plus 38 is 90. That matches the total in the problem, so the first condition is correct.

**Screen check**

The board shows “52 + 38 = 90 bags” and “✓ The total matches.”

## Stop 12: Check the move and the difference

**Action**

Ask, pause, and then select **Next**.

**Ask**

Move 4 bags from A to B again. Do both final counts and their difference match?

**Pause**

Let students calculate the two final counts and then subtract.

**Say**

Box A becomes 52 minus 4, or 48. Box B becomes 38 plus 4, or 42. Forty-eight minus 42 is 6, so Box A really does have 6 more bags after the move.

**Screen check**

The dashed arrow shows the check. The board reads “52 − 4 = 48, 38 + 4 = 42” and “48 − 42 = 6 bags ✓.”

## Stop 13: State the complete answer

**Action**

Ask, pause, and then select **Next**.

**Ask**

Both conditions match. What is the complete answer?

**Pause**

Ask for a full sentence naming both original counts.

**Say**

Answer: Box A originally had 52 bags, and Box B originally had 38 bags.

**Screen check**

The original two-box scene remains visible. The screen shows both check marks and the complete answer. The primary button now says **Replay**.
