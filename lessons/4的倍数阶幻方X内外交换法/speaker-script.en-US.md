# Multiples-of-4 Magic Squares: Swap Inside or Outside X

- Grade: Grades 5–6
- Lesson path: `lessons/4的倍数阶幻方X内外交换法/index.html`
- Suggested duration: 22–27 minutes
- Default parameter: `4 × 4`; switchable to `8 × 8`, `12 × 12`, and `16 × 16`
- Teacher cue: Teach the `4 × 4` case fully. At Stops 4 and 6, **Swap next pair** swaps exactly one pair. Stops 11–13 use a fixed `4 × 4` example to explain the principle.

## Stop 1: Read the task

**Action**

Open the lesson and remain on the opening screen.

**Ask**

Which numbers must go into the square? Which lines must have equal totals?

**Pause**

Let students restate the task.

**Say**

We will use 1 through 16 once each in a 4 by 4 square. Rows, columns, and both diagonals must match. First understand the task; do not swap anything yet.

**Screen check**

The full task is on the left and a blank 4 by 4 square is on the right. There is no colour grouping, X, or number yet.

## Stop 2: Fill the square in order

**Action**

Select **Next**.

**Ask**

In what order should the numbers enter the square?

**Pause**

Wait for “left to right, then top to bottom.”

**Say**

Do not look for the pattern yet. Fill 1, 2, 3, and on through 16: move left to right across a row, then start the next row.

**Screen check**

Numbers appear in order until the ordinary sequential square is full. There is no X, colour grouping, or swap yet.

## Stop 3: Draw X and find centre partners

**Action**

Select **Next**.

**Ask**

Which cells are inside and outside X? Across the centre, which number is opposite 1?

**Pause**

Invite two partner pairs.

**Say**

Only now draw the pale dashed X. Inside-X and outside-X cells receive two background colours. Opposite cells are partners: 1 and 16, 2 and 15, and so on. Every pair totals 17.

**Screen check**

The pale dashed X appears. Numbers stay in place while inside-X and outside-X cells receive different backgrounds.

## Stop 4: Swap partners inside X

**Action**

Use **Swap next pair** repeatedly.

**Ask**

Where will the blue partner pair move?

**Pause**

Pause after each swap.

**Say**

Swap only the four pairs inside X: 1 and 16, 4 and 13, 6 and 11, 7 and 10. Everything outside X stays still, and finished swaps turn green.

**Screen check**

Only one blue pair changes place at a time; completed values turn green.

## Stop 5: Inspect the first square

**Action**

Select **Next**.

**Ask**

Can the first row make 34?

**Pause**

Let students add it mentally.

**Say**

The inside-X swaps make the first magic square. We will check every kind of line at the end.

**Screen check**

The completed inside-X square and magic sum are visible.

## Stop 6: Swap partners outside X

**Action**

Select **Next**, then use **Swap next pair** repeatedly.

**Ask**

There is another way. If X stays still, which four outside pairs swap?

**Pause**

Let students name 2↔15, 3↔14, 5↔12, and 8↔9.

**Say**

This is the other choice: keep X and swap outside partners. It is still the same centre-partner rule, and finished swaps turn green.

**Screen check**

One outside-X pair is blue at a time; finished swaps are green.

## Stop 7: Inspect the second square

**Action**

Select **Next**.

**Ask**

Does this arrangement look identical to the first one?

**Pause**

Let students compare.

**Say**

It is arranged differently, but it is also a magic square. The two results form a symmetric pair.

**Screen check**

The completed outside-X square is visible.

## Stop 8: See the rotation

**Action**

Select **Next**.

**Ask**

What happens if the first square turns halfway around?

**Pause**

Ask students to predict before the turn.

**Say**

Turning the first square 180 degrees makes the second square. Swapping inside X and outside X are two choices in one symmetric rule.

**Screen check**

For orders 4 and 8, both squares and the rotation arrow are visible. For orders 12 and 16, one readable square remains with an arrow that names the 180-degree turn to the other answer.

## Stop 9: Extend the rule

**Action**

Select **Next**. Optionally choose `8 × 8`, `12 × 12`, or `16 × 16` in settings.

**Ask**

How many 4 by 4 blocks fit in an 8 by 8 square? What is drawn in each one?

**Pause**

Let students point to the X marks.

**Say**

A multiple-of-4 square is built from 4 by 4 blocks. Draw the same X in every block, then consistently choose inside-X or outside-X partners.

**Screen check**

After switching to order 8, 12, or 16, every 4 by 4 block shows X. Higher orders show the active partner and the completed-pair count instead of a long pair list.

## Stop 10: Verify every kind of line

**Action**

Select **Next**, then use **Check next line**.

**Ask**

Do this row, this column, and both diagonals all equal 34?

**Pause**

Pause before each displayed sum.

**Say**

Rows, columns, and diagonals all equal the magic sum. Remember the structure, not two disconnected answers: centre partners plus one choice, inside X or outside X.

**Screen check**

The matching row, column, or diagonal on the left square turns green, and its displayed sum equals the magic sum.

## Stop 11: Partner swaps do not repeat numbers

**Action**

Select **Next**.

**Ask**

After 1 and 16 trade places, can any number be repeated or missing?

**Pause**

Listen for “they only change places.”

**Say**

1 and 16 are centre partners and total 17. Every pair only trades places, so no number is added, removed, or repeated.

**Screen check**

The 4 by 4 example highlights 1 and 16, alongside `1 ↔ 16` and `1 + 16 = 17`.

## Stop 12: Why this X?

**Action**

Select **Next**.

**Ask**

Why cannot we draw any random X?

**Pause**

Have students choose a row or column and count inside-X and outside-X cells.

**Say**

In every 4 by 4 block, each row and column has exactly two X-cells and two outside-X cells. A larger square simply tiles this small structure.

**Screen check**

The 4 by 4 example shows the pale dashed X and the two cell backgrounds.

## Stop 13: A row is filled up to the magic sum

**Action**

Select **Next**.

**Ask**

How does the first row change from 10 to the magic sum, 34?

**Pause**

Ask students to find the 1 and 4 inside X on the first row.

**Say**

Before swapping, `1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10`. The inside-X 1 and 4 trade for 16 and 13, giving `16 + 2 + 3 + 13 = 34`. Early rows receive larger partners and later rows receive smaller ones. Both diagonals lie on X, so swapping every partner on a diagonal also preserves the total.

**Screen check**

The first row of the completed 4 by 4 square is green, with both before and after sums shown on the right.
