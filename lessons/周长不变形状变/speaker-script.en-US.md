# Geometry Canvas | Teacher Script

Grade: 3–4. Lesson path: `lessons/周长不变形状变/index.html`. Suggested time: 8–12 minutes. The canvas starts empty, with 4 cm in the segment field. Any positive length can be entered, and there is no fixed limit on the number of segments. **Action** lines are teacher cues. There are no page steps; use these three stops at the child's pace.

## Stop 1: Set lengths and arrange segments

**Action**

Enter 4 in “Length” and click “+ Segment” four times, or leave the input and press N. The “Shortcuts ?” button lists all keys. Each grid cell represents 1 cm. Open the “Style” panel and click “Hide lengths” and “Show lengths”; each segment displays its number without a unit. With “Segment” mode selected, grab the body to move one piece. Drag a round endpoint diagonally to turn it; connected pieces follow. Drag along the segment to change its length in a separate experiment. Angles gently snap near horizontal and vertical, while other angles remain available.

**Ask**

Each segment measures 4 cm. Does turning it change its length?

**Pause**

Let the child move one piece by its body, then turn it by dragging its endpoint diagonally. Read the unchanged length label.

**Say**

We are moving the segments, not stretching or cutting them. The outline is still open, so the screen shows the total length of the segments. We do not call that a perimeter yet.

**Screen check**

All four pieces span four cells and display the number 4 without a unit. The slim status bar below the canvas shows a total of 16 cm; the perimeter line still says “Close the outline.” The numbers disappear and return with the toggle. Zero is rejected with a message in the status bar; a 50 cm segment spans 50 cells. Scroll on the canvas with a trackpad or mouse wheel to pan beyond the viewport. Hold Ctrl and scroll, or use the zoom controls in the lower-right corner, to zoom in and out; “Fit” frames all the content. Shapes do not shrink automatically.

## Stop 2: Close the outline

**Action**

Arrange segments end to end and watch nearby endpoints snap together. The perimeter appears in the status bar when all four sides make one closed outline. Or click “+ Shape”: choose “Square” with side 4 and set “Quantity” to 3. The three squares appear at separate positions and can then be moved individually. “Rectangle” takes a length and width; “Triangle” takes three sides; “Trapezoid” takes two bases and two legs. “Circle” takes a radius; “Ellipse” takes horizontal and vertical radii.

**Ask**

When can the total length become the perimeter of a shape?

**Pause**

Ask the child to trace the closed boundary once and return to the start.

**Say**

When the segments join end to end and close, the distance all the way around is the perimeter. Four sides of 4 cm make 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 = 16 cm.

**Screen check**

The selected closed shape shows its name and perimeter in the status bar. Each of the three squares with side 4 cm has a 16 cm perimeter; a 6 by 3 cm rectangle has an 18 cm perimeter. Circles and ellipses show approximate perimeters. Open segments show only their total length.

## Stop 3: Make another shape and compare

**Action**

Select a shape and click “Shape” mode or press 2; dragging a side moves the connected group. You can also drag inside a closed shape to move it directly. Switch to “Segment” mode or press 1 to move only one side. Drag a round joint diagonally to reshape connected pieces. Drag a box from empty canvas space to select several objects and move them together; Ctrl+A selects all objects. Use “Copy” and “Paste” in the “More” menu, or Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V, to place a duplicate elsewhere; use “Undo” or Ctrl+Z to reverse the last edit. With an object selected, open the “Style” panel to choose from ten common colors or a custom color and change “Line width,” “Line style,” and “Endpoint size.” Click “Delete” or press Delete; a selected side removes only that side, while a selected whole shape removes the group. Click “Connect” or press 3, then drag out from any endpoint to add a new segment; it snaps to nearby endpoints, so a dashed side can close an open outline. With objects selected, click “Save shape” to keep them in the library; next time, open “Library” and choose “Insert” to reuse them. “Save whole canvas” in the “More” menu opens a name field before storing everything. Use “Rename” in the library to change a saved item's name later.

Select a 12 cm segment and choose “More → Split by length.” Split it equally into three 4 cm parts, or enter 2, 3, and 7 cm. Press 4 or click “Click to split,” then click anywhere inside a segment to make a new endpoint. Switch to “Connect” to draw from that point. Hold Shift to select several touching segments on the same straight line, then choose “More → Merge segments” or press J. A junction with another branch stays in place.

Select a closed shape, open “Style,” and use the ten swatches under “Fill color” or the color input to fill it. “Clear fill” makes it transparent. Hold Ctrl while dragging a segment to move along the first dominant horizontal or vertical direction. Hold Ctrl while connecting to use whole centimetres; hold it while dragging an endpoint along a segment to change its length in 1 cm steps. Release Ctrl to arrange or rotate freely.

If a new segment divides a square into left and right spaces, leave “Connect” mode and click inside each space to choose its own fill. The page shows the perimeter of the clicked space; without a space selected, it still shows the connected group's total segment length.

**Ask**

The shape and angles changed. Which side lengths stayed the same? What about the distance around?

**Pause**

Let the child read every side label and add them aloud.

**Say**

Dragging a segment body or turning an endpoint diagonally changes positions and angles while keeping side lengths. The same pieces still make the entire boundary, so the perimeter stays the same. A square can become a rhombus; a rectangle can become a parallelogram. Dragging an endpoint along its segment changes that side length and may change the perimeter, so use the turning gesture for this demonstration.

**Screen check**

The square and rhombus both show 16 cm. The rectangle and parallelogram both show 18 cm. Color, width, and dashes change the drawing without changing any length or perimeter. Moving or deleting one side opens that shape, so its perimeter disappears until the outline closes again.
