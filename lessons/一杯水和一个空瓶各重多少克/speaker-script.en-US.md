# How Much Do One Cup of Water and the Empty Bottle Weigh? — Speaker Script

- Grade: 3
- Lesson path: `lessons/一杯水和一个空瓶各重多少克/index.html`
- Suggested duration: 10–12 minutes
- Default values: 3 cups with the bottle weigh 440 g; 5 cups with the bottle weigh 600 g
- Teacher cue: Do not read the **Action** notes aloud. After each click, let the visual change finish before continuing.

## Parameter branches

- **Default branch:** Use 3 cups and 440 g, then 5 cups and 600 g. One cup of water weighs 80 g, and the empty bottle weighs 200 g.
- **Other valid values:** The page places the weighing with fewer cups on the left and the weighing with more cups on the right. Read only the values and equations currently shown.
- **Reversed input order:** If the first weighing has more cups, the left label says “Second weighing: fewer cups,” while the right label says “First weighing: more cups.” The reasoning stays the same.
- **Invalid branch:** The settings window remains open and explains the problem when the cup counts match, more cups do not give more weight, the weight difference cannot be shared evenly, or the calculated bottle weight is not positive. Do not teach from an invalid state.

## Stop 1: Read both weighing records

**Action**

Open the lesson and stay on Step 1, “Read both weighing records.” Do not click **Next step** yet.

**Ask**

What do we know, and what do we need to find?

**Pause**

Wait for students to name both cup counts, both total weights, and the two unknown weights.

**Say**

We use the same cup to pour water into the same empty bottle. With 3 cups of water, the bottle and water weigh 440 grams. With 5 cups, they weigh 600 grams. We need the weight of one cup of water and the weight of the empty bottle. For now, we are only making sure we understand the problem.

**Screen check**

The stage shows the complete problem, 3 cups with 440 g, 5 cups with 600 g, and the exact question. No method, 80 g, or 200 g is visible.

## Stop 2: Line up the matching parts

**Action**

Click **Next step** to open “Line up the matching parts.”

**Ask**

What is exactly the same in the 3-cup and 5-cup weighings?

**Pause**

Wait for students to identify the same empty bottle and the first 3 matching cups of water.

**Say**

Line up the two records. The 3 cups on the left match the first 3 cups on the right. The empty bottle is also the same. These matching parts cannot be the cause of the difference between the two total weights.

**Screen check**

Every cup of water has the same height. The 3 units on the left align with the lower 3 units on the right and share one highlight, while the extra 2 units are muted. Only “Same: bottle + 3 cups” remains in the center; no weight difference, equation, or answer is shown.

## Stop 3: Find the extra weight

**Action**

Click **Next step** to open “Find the extra weight.”

**Ask**

How many more grams does the 5-cup weighing show?

**Pause**

Wait for students to calculate `600 − 440`.

**Say**

The weighing with more cups is 600 grams. The one with fewer cups is 440 grams. Six hundred minus four hundred forty is 160 grams. That is the extra weight in the second record.

**Screen check**

Both scale displays are emphasized, and the board shows `600 − 440 = 160 g`. The two requested weights are still hidden.

## Stop 4: Find the extra cups

**Action**

Click **Next step** to open “Find the extra cups.”

**Ask**

Which water accounts for the extra 160 grams?

**Pause**

Wait while the matching bottle and matching water become quieter, then ask students to identify the 2 extra cups.

**Say**

We can remove the same empty bottle from both records. We can also remove the same 3 cups of water. The second record still has `5 − 3 = 2` cups left. Those 2 extra cups must weigh the extra 160 grams.

**Screen check**

The matching parts are de-emphasized. The 2 extra water units turn gold and move into the comparison area. The board shows `600 − 440 = 160 g` and `5 − 3 = 2 cups`.

## Stop 5: Find one cup's weight

**Action**

Click **Next step** to open “Find one cup's weight.”

**Ask**

If 2 cups weigh 160 grams, how much does one cup weigh?

**Pause**

Wait for students to explain that 160 grams must be shared equally between 2 cups.

**Say**

The two cups hold equal amounts of water, so divide 160 grams equally between them. `160 ÷ 2 = 80`. One cup of water weighs 80 grams.

**Screen check**

The two gold water units separate, “80 g each” appears, and the board shows `160 ÷ 2 = 80 g`.

## Stop 6: Find the weight of 3 cups

**Action**

Click **Next step** to open “Find the weight of 3 cups.”

**Ask**

How much do the 3 cups in the lighter weighing weigh altogether?

**Pause**

Wait for students to form `80 × 3`.

**Say**

One cup weighs 80 grams, so 3 cups are three groups of 80 grams. `80 × 3 = 240`. The water in the lighter weighing weighs 240 grams altogether.

**Screen check**

The 3 water units in the left bottle become the focus. The board shows `80 × 3 = 240 g` and “3 cups weigh 240 g.”

## Stop 7: Find the empty bottle's weight

**Action**

Click **Next step** to open “Find the empty bottle's weight.”

**Ask**

The lighter weighing totals 440 grams. What remains after removing the water's 240 grams?

**Pause**

Wait for students to calculate `440 − 240` and name the remaining object.

**Say**

The bottle and 3 cups weigh 440 grams together. The water weighs 240 grams. Remove the water's weight: `440 − 240 = 200`. The remaining 200 grams is the weight of the empty bottle.

**Screen check**

The water becomes quieter, the bottle outline turns gold, and the board shows `440 − 240 = 200 g`. The bottle is labeled “Empty bottle: 200 g.”

## Stop 8: Check both records

**Action**

Click **Next step** to open “Check both records.” After the explanation, use **Restart lesson** if you want to begin again.

**Ask**

Do 80 grams for one cup and 200 grams for the bottle make both original records true?

**Pause**

Let students check the 3-cup and 5-cup records separately before stating the complete answer.

**Say**

Check the first record: `200 + 3 × 80 = 440` grams. Check the second record: `200 + 5 × 80 = 600` grams. Both totals match the problem. One cup of water weighs 80 grams, and the empty bottle weighs 200 grams.

**Screen check**

Both bottle outlines and scale displays turn green. Two checked equations appear, followed by the complete answer.
